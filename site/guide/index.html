How to work out how many recessed lights you need

Good recessed lighting balances total brightness with even ceiling spacing. Use this walkthrough to pick a sensible target light level for the room, calculate fixture count, and check grid spacing.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure room length and width in feet or meters to find the floor area.
  2. Choose a target illuminance in foot-candles (US) or lux (metric) matching how the room is used—work kitchens need far more light than bedrooms.
  3. Read the delivered lumen rating of one recessed fixture from its product specification sheet rather than relying on wattage.
  4. Set a utilization fraction (commonly 0.65 to 0.75 for typical residential ceilings and walls) to reflect light lost before reaching the working plane.
  5. Check the suggested rectangular grid spacing against your ceiling height; keeping spacing at or below half the ceiling height helps avoid shadowed spots.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length: Longest room dimension.
  • Room width: Shorter room dimension.
  • Target light level: Foot-candles (US) or lux (metric); see room table below.
  • Light output per fixture: Delivered lumens listed on one recessed LED downlight.
  • Utilization fraction: Share of light reaching the working plane (typically 0.65 to 0.75).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using an old "ceiling height divided by two" spacing rule without checking whether the fixtures actually output enough lumens for the task.
  • Comparing LED downlights by watts or "60W equivalent" marketing copy instead of rated lumens and color temperature.
  • Placing downlights too close to cabinets or too far from kitchen counters, which casts your own shadow over the work surface.
  • Ignoring ceiling joists, insulation-contact (IC) ratings, damp/wet location ratings, and local electrical code requirements.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Place outer downlights roughly half the fixture-to-fixture spacing from each wall (about 2 to 3 ft in standard rooms).
  • Pair general recessed grids with under-cabinet or task lighting in kitchens and desks.
  • Confirm spacing, beam angle, and circuit limits with fixture documentation and a licensed electrician.
