How to work out how many recessed lights you need

Estimate fixture count from room area, your target illuminance and fixture light output. Compare the minimum count with a simple rectangular grid; this is an early layout estimate.

Quick answer: A 5 × 4 m room at 150 lux, 800 lm per fixture and 0.70 utilization gives 6 fixtures and a possible 3 × 2 layout.

Step by step

  1. Measure the room length and width to find the floor area.
  2. Choose a target light level for how the room is used. Kitchens and work areas usually need more light than bedrooms or living rooms.
  3. Read the light output of one fixture in lumens from its specification sheet.
  4. Set the share of light that reaches the floor or work surface. The calculator default is a planning assumption; a fixture photometric report gives a better figure.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length
  • Room width
  • Target illuminance
  • Light output per fixture
  • Utilization fraction

How the calculation works

Multiply room area in square metres by the target in lux to obtain lumens needed on the target plane. Divide by fixture lumens multiplied by the utilization fraction and round up. The grid uses enough rows and columns to cover that count; it may contain more fixtures than the minimum.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the count from a spacing rule alone without checking how much light each fixture gives.
  • Comparing fixtures by watts or "equivalent" watts instead of lumens.
  • Checking only the total. Even with enough light, uneven spacing can leave dark patches between fixtures.

Another worked example

A 4 × 4 m room at 100 lux with 1,000 lm fixtures and utilization 1 gives a minimum of 2.

What changes the estimate

The target value is an illustrative input, not a lighting standard. Utilization represents light reaching the target plane and depends on the room and luminaire. Verify spacing, beam angle, glare, installation clearances and electrical requirements with the fixture documentation and a qualified installer. Grid spacing uses half-spacing offsets at the room edges.
